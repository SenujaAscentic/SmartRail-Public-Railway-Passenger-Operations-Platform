# Session occasional-commuter-2026-10-06T05-04-42-611Z

Topic: Describe a typical trip you take right now, before SmartRail, and how often you travel: how you plan it, what you do at the station and on the train, what goes wrong and how often.

**moderator**

@occasional-commuter: When you encounter a delay or a service disruption during your trip, what is the first source you turn to for information, and how effective is that information in helping you decide whether to wait or find an alternative route?

---

**occasional-commuter**

Honestly, when there’s a delay, I feel stuck. I usually look for a station announcement, but half the time the speakers are crackling or the train is so loud I can’t hear a word. If I’m lucky, I see a small group of people heading for the exit, so I follow them, but I have no idea if they’re going to a bus stop or just giving up.

The brief doesn't mention how we get info when the power goes out or the speakers fail—which happens! I don't want technical jargon about "service status updates." I need to know: *Is my train coming in 10 minutes or two hours?* And if it's cancelled, *what is the exact alternative?*

If I'm at Colombo Fort and my train to Galle is cancelled, I don't know the bus routes. I just want the app to tell me, "The 14:00 is cancelled; take the 14:30 instead," or "Walk to the bus stand for a coach to Panadura." Right now, I'm just guessing, and that’s terrifying when I have luggage and a friend with me.

---

**moderator**

@occasional-commuter: You mentioned the anxiety of traveling with luggage and companions; if the app provides an alternative route, such as a bus connection, what specific information do you need to feel confident enough to leave the station rather than staying put and hoping the train service resumes?

---

**occasional-commuter**

If I’m leaving the station to catch a bus, my biggest fear is getting stranded somewhere else or missing the bus because I didn’t know where to look.

To feel confident, I need three things:

1. **Exact directions:** Don’t just say "take a bus." Tell me exactly where the bus stop is relative to the station exit. "Turn left at the main gate, walk 200 meters past the taxi stand."
2. **Confidence in the alternative:** Is that bus actually going to get me to my destination, or will I be stuck in traffic for hours? If the app says, "This bus is the best alternative," I need to know it’s a reliable option, not just a random suggestion.
3. **The "What if":** If I get to the bus stop and that’s delayed too, what then?

If I have luggage, I can’t be running around trying to find a bus stand that might not even have space. I need to know the bus is a real, viable plan. If the app just gives me a list of buses without telling me which one is actually the safest or fastest for a traveler, I’ll probably just stand there and wait for the train, even if it’s hours late.

---

**moderator**

@occasional-commuter: You mentioned the fear of being stranded if the alternative bus is also delayed or full; in a scenario where you've followed the app's directions to a bus stop but find it overcrowded or stalled, what is the minimum level of "system accountability" you would need from the app to prevent you from feeling completely abandoned in that moment?

---

**occasional-commuter**

If I’m standing at a bus stop with my bags and the bus is nowhere to be seen, I feel completely abandoned. "System accountability" to me isn't a complex feature—it’s just the app acting like a human guide who hasn't given up on me.

I need the app to know I'm there. If it suggested the bus, it should know if that route is failing. I need a "Plan C" button that actually works. If I press it, I don't want a list of technical schedules; I want it to say, "The bus is delayed. A train is now expected at the station in 20 minutes—walk back to Platform 2." 

Or, if there’s no train, give me a "Get Help" option. Even if it’s just a clear button to call a station official or a verified taxi service that knows where I am. Basically, don't just dump a suggestion on me and go silent. If the app leads me into a bad situation, it has to be the one to help me find the way out of it.

---

**moderator**

MODERATOR: @occasional-commuter: Following that "Plan C" concept, if the app directs you back to the station or to a taxi, how do you envision the transition of responsibility—specifically, how would you confirm that the "Help" or "Plan C" you've requested is actually being honored by the station staff or the taxi service so you aren't just standing there waiting for a promise that might not materialize?

---

**occasional-commuter**

I need to know the app isn't just a screen of text; it needs to be connected to the real world. 

If I’m being sent back to the station, I need a digital "ticket" or a reference code on my phone that the staff at the gate actually recognizes. It shouldn't be a generic message; it should say, "Your seat is reserved on the 16:00 train to Galle—show this to the platform guard." That way, I know I’m not just walking back into a crowd where nobody knows I’m coming.

For taxis, I need a status update—like a little map showing the car is actually moving toward my location, just like those ride-hailing apps people use. If the app tells me "a taxi is coming," I need to see it, or at least have a driver's name and a confirmed arrival time. 

If I can’t see the progress, I’m just standing there with my luggage, worrying. Accountability means the app gives me proof that someone is actually on their way to help, so I can stop guessing and start trusting the system.

---

**moderator**

MODERATOR: @occasional-commuter: You’ve highlighted the need for real-time, verified accountability, but what happens when technology fails you—for instance, if your phone battery dies or the station has no cellular data coverage while you are stranded? How do you expect the "system" to maintain this level of support when your primary link to the app is broken?

---

**occasional-commuter**

That is exactly what keeps me up at night! If my phone dies or the signal drops at a place like Maradana, I feel like I’ve lost my only lifeline. 

If the app is my guide, it can't just be a "digital-only" thing. When the technology fails, I need the station to be prepared for people like me. I need big, clear physical boards that actually show the same info the app was telling me—not those old, static signs that never get updated. 

Also, if the app *knows* I’m in a middle of a journey and the signal drops, the station staff should have a way to help me. Maybe there’s a "Help Point" at the station where I can just show my ticket or a printed reference, and they can see my status on their end. 

If I can't use the app, I shouldn't be treated like a stranger. The system needs to be "smart" enough that the staff are informed about the disruptions too, so they aren't just shrugging their shoulders when I ask them what’s happening. Technology is great, but it can’t be the *only* way to get home.
