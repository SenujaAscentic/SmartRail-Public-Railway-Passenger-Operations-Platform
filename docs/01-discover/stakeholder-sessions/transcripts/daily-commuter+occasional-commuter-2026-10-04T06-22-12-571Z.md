# Session daily-commuter+occasional-commuter-2026-10-04T06-22-12-571Z

Topic: How much guidance should the app show?

**moderator**

As a daily commuter who likely knows your route by heart, under what specific conditions or disruptions would you want the app to actively intervene with step-by-step guidance, rather than remaining in a quiet, background state?

---

**daily-commuter**

Look, I don't need "step-by-step guidance" on how to walk to my usual platform. I do this every single day. Keep the app quiet unless my specific, saved commute is falling apart.

I only want the app to actively intervene under three exact conditions:

1. **Sudden cancellations or delays over 10 minutes:** If my 17:15 is dead, I need a push notification *immediately*—while I'm still at my desk—telling me what to do. Is my season ticket valid on the slow service? Is there a replacement bus? I can't miss my kid's school pickup.
2. **Last-minute platform changes:** If I’m standing in a packed corridor, tell me the platform change instantly so I can move before the crowd rushes the stairs. 
3. **Direct alternatives:** Don't give me a map or a tutorial. Just tell me: *"Your train is cancelled. Take the 17:25 from Platform 4 instead."* 

If everything is running normally, stay completely out of my way. No onboarding, no "discover" features, no clutter. One tap to see my status, and that's it.

---

**moderator**

Occasional-commuter, while the daily-commuter wants a completely stripped-back, quiet app with no onboarding or maps, your experience is likely very different. When you are navigating a large, unfamiliar station—especially during a disruption—what specific level of step-by-step guidance or wayfinding do you actually need from the app to feel confident?

---

**occasional-commuter**

I have to completely disagree with the daily commuter. If the app is stripped back to almost nothing, I will be completely lost. I don't know these stations by heart! 

When I'm carrying heavy luggage, just telling me "Go to Platform 4" isn't enough. Where *is* Platform 4? Is there a lift? I need a simple, visual layout of the station so I can find my way without panicking. 

During a disruption, I need reassurance. I want step-by-step guidance: which train to take, exactly where to change, and how many stops are left. I need the app to tell me *on the screen* that I am indeed on the right train. 

Please don't use confusing railway jargon or abbreviations either. I don't know what they mean! And I definitely don't want to be forced to create an account just to look up a single journey and see the total ticket price, including seat booking. 

For people like me, a "quiet" app is an empty, scary app. We need visual help and clear, plain-language directions to feel safe.

---

**moderator**

Daily-commuter, since the occasional commuter needs a highly visual, guided interface, how should the app determine that you are an "expert" who wants the stripped-back, quiet experience, without forcing you through the very onboarding or setup screens you want to avoid?

---

**daily-commuter**

Look, it is simple. Don't force me through a ten-step setup or make me create an account. When I open the app for the first time, just give me a massive, obvious button that says "Skip to Commute" and let me search my route and pin it immediately. 

If the other guy wants maps, station layouts, and step-by-step hand-holding, let him tap a "Guide Me" button. But don't clutter *my* home screen with it. When I'm running down the platform with weak signal in the pouring rain, I can't be waiting for a heavy station map to load and drain my battery. 

I just need to see: *"17:15 to Croydon - On Time - Platform 3."* If I save that route once, that should be the *only* thing I see when I open the app. No welcome tutorials, no "discover" tabs, no fluff. Just my train, right there, instantly. 

The brief completely leaves out offline usability. Half the time on my platform, I have zero signal. If the app requires a constant, heavy connection to load all those fancy visual guides, it’s going to fail me when I need it most.

---

**moderator**

Occasional-commuter, since you rely heavily on visual station maps and step-by-step reassurance to navigate, how should the app assist you when you lose cellular signal inside a deep underground station or a crowded terminal where those heavy visual guides might fail to load?

---

**occasional-commuter**

Oh, losing signal is absolutely terrifying when you are already stressed and carrying heavy bags! If I lose connection and the app just spins, shows an error, or goes completely blank, I will panic. That happens to me all the time with other apps—they just forget my journey the second the signal drops, leaving me stranded.

I don’t need a fancy, heavy video or a 3D map that takes forever to load. But I *do* need the app to automatically save my step-by-step plan, the list of stops, and a simple, basic drawing of where the lifts are the moment I search for my journey. It should stay on my screen even when I'm deep underground. 

If the connection dies, the app must reassure me by saying, "You are offline, but here is your saved plan." Don't hide my platform number or my stops behind a loading spinner. The brief completely leaves out how scary it is to be left in the dark with zero information when the internet cuts out. We need that reassurance to stay on our screens, no matter what.
