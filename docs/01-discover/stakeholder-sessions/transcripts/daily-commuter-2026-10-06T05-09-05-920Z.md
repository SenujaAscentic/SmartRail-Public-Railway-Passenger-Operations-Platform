# Session daily-commuter-2026-10-06T05-09-05-920Z

Topic: Describe your normal day and your normal week on the train right now, before SmartRail: hour by hour what you do, which apps, boards or people you rely on, what goes wrong and how often.

**moderator**

What is the very first thing you do to check the status of your train before you leave your home, and what time does that usually happen?

---

**daily-commuter**

I leave my house in Gampaha at 6:15 AM sharp. The very first thing I do is open the RDMNS app while I’m still drinking my tea. I don't look at anything else—I just tap my saved route to Colombo Fort and look for the status of the 6:40 AM express.

Honestly, it’s a nervous habit. I’m looking for a green "on time" light, but half the time it’s just a grey box because the data hasn't refreshed or the signal in my area is acting up. If it says there’s a delay, I start stressing immediately about whether I’ll make it to the office in time for my first meeting. If the app is grey or feels like it's hanging, I just put my phone in my pocket and hope for the best, because I have to leave anyway to catch the bus to the station. I don't have time to mess around with it; if I miss that 6:40, my whole day is ruined.

---

**moderator**

Once you arrive at Gampaha station for the 6:40 AM train, how do you confirm which platform your train is arriving on, and do you rely on any specific boards or announcements for that information?

---

**daily-commuter**

Once I reach Gampaha station, it’s usually a mad rush. I don’t even bother checking my phone anymore because the signal near the tracks is terrible and the RDMNS app doesn't show platforms anyway. It’s useless for that.

I head straight for the main station notice board, but honestly, you can't always trust it. If the board says one thing and the station master or the guards are shouting something else, I follow the crowd. I listen for the PA system, but half the time it’s just crackling static or the announcement is so garbled in the noise of the station that you can’t make out a word. 

If I see everyone shifting toward a specific platform, I just move with them. It’s stressful because you’re constantly looking over your shoulder to see if people are running the other way. If I’m really unsure, I’ll just ask a fellow commuter who looks like they take the same train every day. We all look out for each other because the official boards are often behind the reality of what’s actually happening on the line.

---

**moderator**

When you finish your workday and head back to Colombo Fort station for your trip home, what is the first thing you do to check the status of your return train?

---

**daily-commuter**

When I finish at the office, usually around 5:00 PM, I don’t even bother with the app immediately. I walk into Colombo Fort and go straight to the big electronic boards in the main concourse. That’s the first thing I do.

I look for my train heading back to Gampaha. If the board is working, it shows the time and the platform, but you know how it is—sometimes it just says "Delayed" without telling you why or for how long. If I see that, I look for a station official or a guard to ask, "Is the train actually coming, or is it cancelled?" 

Only after I’ve checked the board and gotten a sense of the mood in the station do I pull out my phone to check RDMNS. I’m mostly looking to see if other passengers have posted any "live" reports about the train being stuck somewhere up the line. But I take those reports with a pinch of salt. If the app says it’s coming but the board doesn't show a platform, I’m stuck standing there, watching the clock and hoping I don't miss my kid's pickup. It’s a constant guessing game.

---

**moderator**

How does your routine at the station change on weekends or public holidays compared to your typical weekday commute?

---

**daily-commuter**

On weekends or Poya days, my routine changes completely because the schedule isn't the same. I don't have the luxury of knowing the 6:40 AM like the back of my hand, so I don't even try to rely on my usual habits.

If I have to go into Colombo on a Saturday, I don't bother with the RDMNS app because the crowdsourced reports are thin—there aren't enough regular commuters posting updates on those days. Instead, I just head to Gampaha station earlier than I think I need to. I know I’ll be standing on the platform for a while, so I just wait for the station master to confirm the next train over the loudspeaker. 

Honestly, on holidays, I just expect delays. I don't stress as much because I don't have that 8:30 AM meeting hanging over my head, but it’s annoying. I just stand there, watch the boards, and wait. It’s less of a "rush to the office" feeling and more of a "just get me there eventually" situation. I leave the app alone; it’s not worth the battery drain when the trains are running on a holiday schedule anyway.

---

**moderator**

What is the specific issue that goes wrong most often in a typical week, how frequently does it happen, and how do you spend that time while you are waiting for it to be sorted out?

---

**daily-commuter**

The biggest issue is the "indefinite delay." It happens at least twice a week—either a signal failure or a technical breakdown somewhere up the line. The train just stops or doesn't show up, and the information disappears.

When it happens, I’m usually trapped on the platform at Colombo Fort or stuck on the train itself. The worst part is the silence. No one tells you if the delay is ten minutes or two hours. I spend that time glued to my phone, refreshing RDMNS, hoping someone further up the line has posted a status. But most of the time, I’m just standing there, feeling my blood pressure rise as I think about the school pickup.

I end up calling my wife to tell her I’ll be late, then I call my manager, which is always an awkward, tense conversation. I just stand there, often in the heat, watching the station board flicker or stay blank. I’ve become an expert at people-watching and listening to the grumbling of the crowd. We all just stand there, tired, checking our watches, waiting for a miracle announcement that tells us we can finally move.
