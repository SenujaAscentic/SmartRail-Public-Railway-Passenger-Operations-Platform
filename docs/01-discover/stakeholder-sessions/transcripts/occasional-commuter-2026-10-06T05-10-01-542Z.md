# Session occasional-commuter-2026-10-06T05-10-01-542Z

Topic: Describe a typical trip you take right now, before SmartRail, and how often you travel: how you plan it, what you do at the station and on the train, what goes wrong and how often.

**moderator**

To start your typical trip, what is the very first thing you do to prepare, and what time does that usually happen?

---

**occasional-commuter**

When I’m planning a trip—say, to visit a friend in Galle or heading up to Kandy for a weekend—the very first thing I do is ask my friends if they know which train is the "good" one. I don't trust the schedules I find online because they are often just lists of times without saying if the train is actually comfortable or if it’s one of those packed commuter ones where you can’t even move.

I usually do this a day or two before, maybe in the evening when I’m home from work. If I can't get a clear answer, I end up feeling really anxious. I’ll go to the station earlier than I probably need to, just because I’m terrified of arriving at Colombo Fort and not knowing which platform to run to. I don't use any specific apps because the ones I've seen look like they were built for engineers, not for someone just trying to get to a destination without getting lost. I just want to know I’m on the right track and that I’ll actually get a seat.

---

**moderator**

Once you arrive at Colombo Fort station, how much time do you typically spend there before your train departs, and what specific steps do you take to confirm you are at the right platform?

---

**occasional-commuter**

I usually aim to get to Colombo Fort at least 45 minutes to an hour before my train leaves. I know that sounds like a lot, but the station is so massive and overwhelming that I’d rather wait on the wrong bench for a while than miss my train entirely.

When I first walk in, I head straight for the main display boards. Honestly, half the time I can’t read them properly because they’re either too small, flickering, or just confusing with all the abbreviations. If I’m lucky, I find a staff member—usually someone in a uniform—and I show them my ticket or just tell them my destination. I have to ask them, "Is this definitely the train to Galle?" or "Which platform is it?" 

If I’m still unsure, I look for someone else who looks like they’re waiting for the same trip and ask them. I don't trust the automated announcements because they’re often muffled or echo too much in the station hall. I basically just stay glued to the platform once I’m told it’s the right one, watching everyone else to see if they look as nervous as I feel!

---

**moderator**

Once you arrive at your destination and get off the train, what is the very first thing you do before you leave the station premises?

---

**occasional-commuter**

The very first thing I do is stop dead in my tracks once I step off the carriage. I usually let the crowd surge past me because I’m always worried about getting pushed or losing my bag in the chaos. I take a deep breath and look around to get my bearings, especially if it’s a station like Galle where the layout isn't immediately obvious.

I check my phone to see if my friend has messaged me about where they are waiting—usually, they’re at the "main gate" or near a specific shop, but I have to figure out where that is relative to where I’m standing. If I’m really lost, I look for the exit signs, though they aren't always clear. I’ll scan the station for a station master’s office or a ticket counter just to be sure I’m heading toward the right exit. I’m always terrified of walking out the wrong side of the station and ending up completely disoriented in a part of town I don't know, especially if it’s starting to get dark.

---

**moderator**

How does your routine change when you travel on a weekday versus a weekend, and are there specific days or times when you find the station significantly more difficult to navigate?

---

**occasional-commuter**

On weekdays, I try to avoid the station entirely during the morning and evening rush hours. I’ve made the mistake of trying to catch a train to Panadura or Veyangoda during peak times before, and it’s a nightmare. The platforms are so packed that you’re basically pushed onto the train by the crowd behind you. It’s impossible to check boards or ask staff for help when everyone is just trying to squeeze into a carriage.

On weekends, it’s much calmer, but it’s harder to predict if a train will be delayed or if there’s a special schedule for a Poya day. I find the station hardest to navigate on those public holidays or long weekends. The crowds are different—lots of families with huge suitcases and people who don’t travel often, just like me. Everyone is confused, the staff seem extra stressed, and the information boards seem to glitch out even more. I feel much more vulnerable then because I can’t rely on following the "regulars" who usually know exactly where to stand. I just stick to the middle of the platform and hope for the best.

---

**moderator**

In a typical week, what is the most common thing that goes wrong during your trip, how often does it happen, how long does it take to sort out, and what specific steps do you take to resolve it?

---

**occasional-commuter**

The most common thing that goes wrong is the train being delayed or, even worse, being told it’s cancelled after I’ve already waited on the platform for ages. This happens to me maybe once every three or four trips.

When it happens, there’s this awful silence where no one knows what to do. I don't get any alerts on my phone, so I just stand there watching the display board for changes. If it stays stuck on "Delayed," I have to go hunt down a staff member. It takes forever because everyone else is doing the same thing. I’ll ask, "Is there another train soon?" or "Should I wait or go to a different platform?"

Sometimes they tell me to just wait, and I end up standing there for another hour or more. If it’s a total cancellation, I usually have to call my friend and tell them I’m stuck. I don't have a backup plan, so I just wait until the next train arrives, even if it’s a slow one that stops at every single station. It’s exhausting and makes me dread the journey home.
