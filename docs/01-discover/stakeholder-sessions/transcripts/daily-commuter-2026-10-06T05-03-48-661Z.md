# Session daily-commuter-2026-10-06T05-03-48-661Z

Topic: Describe your normal day and your normal week on the train right now, before SmartRail: hour by hour what you do, which apps, boards or people you rely on, what goes wrong and how often.

**moderator**

@daily-commuter, when your train is delayed or cancelled during your commute, what is the very first source of information you check, and how often does that source provide you with inaccurate or conflicting information?

---

**daily-commuter**

Look, if I’m on the platform at a place like Gampaha or waiting at Colombo Fort, the first thing I do is look at the physical station boards. But we all know those are hit or miss. If they’re blank or clearly wrong, I open the RDMNS app because it’s the only thing I’ve got.

The problem? It’s constantly conflicting. The app might say my 07:15 is "on time," but the station guard is shouting that it’s cancelled. Or the app shows a delay, but the board says nothing at all. It’s infuriating. I’m standing there in the sun, trying to get a signal, and I have to decide whether to wait or run for a bus. 

If your "SmartRail" system is just another layer of guesswork, don't bother. I need one source of truth. If the app says my train is delayed, I need to know *why* and *for how long* so I can tell my manager I’ll be late. If I can't trust the notification, I’ll just stop opening it. And please, tell me the platform—I'm tired of sprinting across the tracks when they change it at the last minute.

---

**moderator**

@daily-commuter, when that last-minute platform change happens and you are forced to "sprint across the tracks," what specific physical barriers or safety risks do you encounter, and how would you ideally receive that notification to ensure you have enough time to reach the new platform safely?

---

**daily-commuter**

Look, when a platform change happens at a place like Maradana or Fort, it’s chaos. You’ve got hundreds of people pushing through narrow footbridges or trying to cross the tracks—which is dangerous enough. You’re lugging a bag, the sun is blinding, and you’re dodging people while trying to hear an announcement that’s usually muffled or distorted by the noise of the station.

I don’t want to be staring at a screen while I’m navigating a crowd. I need a push notification that hits my phone the *second* the change is logged in your system—not five minutes later when I’m already standing at the wrong side. And make it clear: "Platform 3 changed to Platform 1." Don't make me click through menus to find it.

If I get that alert while I’m still walking toward the platform, I can adjust. But if I’m already stuck in a crush of people on a bridge, no app is going to help me run faster. You need to make sure the station staff and the digital boards are synced with that notification. If the board says one thing and your app says another, I’m going to lose faith in you immediately. Give me one truth, and give it to me early.

---

**moderator**

@daily-commuter, you mentioned the frustration of trying to get a signal in crowded areas; if the station's cellular or Wi-Fi connectivity fails during peak hours, what is your fallback method for accessing real-time travel updates, and does that method currently provide the information you need?

---

**daily-commuter**

Honestly? If the signal dies, I’m stuck. That’s the reality of the commute. My fallback is usually just asking the station master or the guard, but half the time they’re as stressed as we are and don't have the latest info either. Or, I look at the people around me—if everyone starts walking toward the exit, I know the train is cancelled and it’s time to find a bus. It’s primitive, but that’s how we survive the morning.

If SmartRail is going to work, it can't be just another app that hangs the second the network gets busy. You need to make sure the app caches the last known status so I’m not staring at a spinning wheel when I’m underground or in a dead zone. 

And don't suggest I check a website or a social media feed—that’s useless when the data is crawling. I need the app to be lightweight. If I have to wait for a map to load or a dozen photos to download just to see if my train is running, I'll delete it. Keep it simple: text-based, fast, and offline-ready. If it doesn't work when the signal is weak, it's just dead weight on my phone.

---

**moderator**

MODERATOR: @daily-commuter, beyond the status of the train itself, what specific information about the train's *occupancy* would you need to see in the app to decide whether to wait for that train or seek an alternative, and how would you prefer that represented to you at a glance?

---

**daily-commuter**

Look, I don’t need a fancy color-coded map or a percentage bar that’s probably wrong anyway. If I’m at Gampaha, I already know the 07:15 is going to be packed like a tin of sardines—it’s always packed. 

What I need is simple: "Standing room only" or "Seats available." If I’m coming home late after a long shift, knowing if I have to fight for a spot or if I can actually get a seat for the ride to Veyangoda is the difference between a decent evening and a miserable one. 

But don't give me this information if it’s going to make the app heavy. If I have to wait for a "live feed" of the train's interior to load, forget it. Just a small, text-based indicator next to my saved train—that’s all. 

And let me be clear: this only matters if it's accurate. If the app says there’s space but I get on and it’s impossible to even breathe, I’ll stop trusting that feature entirely. Don't waste my time with "estimates." If you can't be sure, just don't show it. Reliability is everything.

---

**moderator**

MODERATOR: @daily-commuter, you've emphasized that reliability is your top priority; if SmartRail were to implement a "crowdsourced" feature where you and other passengers could manually report if a train is actually full or if a platform change has occurred, would you take the time to submit that data, or would you find that to be an added burden during your already stressful commute?

---

**daily-commuter**

Look, I’m an accountant, not a part-time railway employee. My commute is stressful enough as it is—I’m managing school pickups and my manager’s expectations. I don't have the time or the spare battery life to act as a data entry clerk for your system.

If I report a delay, I’m doing it because I’m already frustrated and looking for a way to warn others, but I shouldn't *have* to do that. If your system is relying on me to tell it what’s happening, then you’ve already failed. 

Furthermore, crowdsourcing is exactly why I don't fully trust the RDMNS app right now. It’s just "he-said-she-said." If I’m standing on a platform in the sun, I need official, verified information from the railway, not a guess from some other passenger who might be looking at the wrong train. 

If you want to know if a train is full or if a platform has changed, get that data from the drivers or the station sensors, not from my phone. Don't add buttons to my screen that I have to manage. Keep it simple: show me the truth, don't ask me to write it for you.
