# Conflicts and Ambiguities

Source: stakeholder simulation sessions (`stakeholder-sessions/transcripts/`). Positions are what the AI personas argued, not real stakeholders. Decisions are the author's.

| ID | Conflict | Positions | Status and decision |
|----|----------|-----------|---------------------|
| C-1 | Who can change a platform or declare a closure | Station manager: local veto and local override. Controller: no local override; one source of truth, because a held train blocks the line. | **Decided (partly).** Station staff cannot override train movement or platform assignment. The station sends station information to the controller; the controller assigns the platform and the station is informed. **Decided (author):** the station may post local notices only (platform crowded, keep clear of the stairs); it cannot close a platform or change any train data (A-115). The emergency hold is deferred. |
| C-2 | Platform changes with little notice (under 5 minutes) | Station manager: 15 to 20 minutes needed to move people safely. Controller: sometimes only about 5 minutes. | **Resolved (author's idea).** Hold the train at the new platform and announce calmly, on displays and aloud, that the train will wait for passengers, so people have no reason to run. The wait is promised only when the controller has actually set the hold (A-113). Staff are alerted at the same moment as the public (A-114). The lead time is measured automatically (A-112). The earlier proposal of a 60-second staff-first window is deferred. |
| C-3 | Showing an unverified delay | Daily commuter: show "Delay Likely / Status Unconfirmed" at once; never a false "On Time". Controller: auto-flagging every stopped train causes panic, since red-signal holds are routine. | **Resolved (author).** No unconfirmed status. Lateness comes from the driver GPS and is shown as exact minutes against the schedule ("late by 1 min"), so a stopped train simply shows a growing delay and nothing alarming is announced for a routine red-signal hold. The controller's override (A-27) still applies, and a stale position shows its age or "location unavailable" (A-11). |
| C-4 | Customer-service role in status | Customer service: agents must flag discrepancies and explain delays. Controller: agents cannot change status; approved reason codes only. | **Deferred (author).** Customer service is deferred in the MVP (Section 9). Revisit with it: a "Report Data Error" flag that goes to the controller's queue, never to the public, plus approved multilingual wording. |
| C-5 | Driver reporting | Driver: no screen while moving; one "Incident" button; the controller calls back. Brief: a driver app supporting journey status. | **Decided (author).** The driver picks from a fixed list of incident types; only the guard can add a free-text explanation (A-116). Reports go to the controller, never directly to passengers (S10-05). Queue-and-forward in signal blackspots is already covered by A-55 (offline crew actions are queued with timestamps and sent on reconnect). |
| C-6 | Guest tracking vs account | Occasional commuter: follow a trip without registering. A-07: alerts need an account. | **Decided (author).** A guest can follow one journey on the device for the day, without push alerts. Alerts need an account (A-121). |
| C-7 | Step-free and lift support | Accessibility specialist: lift status, step-free routing, staff escorts. Station manager: tasks on staff handhelds. | **Decided (author).** Lifts are a minor issue (there are stairs too), so none of this is in the MVP. WCAG 2.2 AA (A-100) still applies. |
| C-8 | Food handover at the station | Provider: a runner waits at the train door. Station manager: a safety hazard at the platform edge, and no space or staff to hold food. | **Decided (author).** The provider may assign its own staff at a station, but must inform the station of their details beforehand. Handover happens at places designated by the station; station staff do not hold food (A-128). |
| C-9 | Payment and refunds | Provider and product owner personas: online prepayment and refunds. | **Already decided.** Pay on collection with no refunds in the MVP (A-84, A-95). Loss from cancelled prepared food is a commercial matter (A-127). |
| C-10 | Personal data shared with the provider | Provider and security personas: no personal data at all. A-91, A-110: first name and phone for an active order, with consent. | **Decided (author).** Keep sharing first name and phone for active orders with consent. |
| C-11 | Crew sign-in security vs operational continuity | Security engineer: device binding, biometrics, device management, hardware-backed keys. Driver: a spare device at once, offline sign-in, manual fallback. | **Decided (author).** A-129 and A-130; the heavy controls are deferred. |
| C-12 | Controller sign-off vs automatic alerts | QA and controller: should alerts wait for the controller, and what timeout? | **Already decided.** Only the controller creates disruptions; no unconfirmed or "under review" public state and no timeout (C-3). Unhandled crew reports stay highlighted (A-135). |
