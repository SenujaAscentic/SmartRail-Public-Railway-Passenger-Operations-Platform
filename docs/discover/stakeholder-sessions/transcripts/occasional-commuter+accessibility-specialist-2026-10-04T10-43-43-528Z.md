# Session occasional-commuter+accessibility-specialist-2026-10-04T10-43-43-528Z

Topic: What does a station and journey need so that newcomers and passengers with access needs can navigate?

**moderator**

**occasional-commuter**, when arriving at a station you rarely or never use, what specific step-by-step guidance or information is currently missing that makes it difficult for you to navigate to the correct platform confidently?

---

**occasional-commuter**

When I step into a huge, noisy station like Colombo Fort or Maradana with my luggage, I immediately feel lost. What’s completely missing right now is simple, step-by-step guidance from the station entrance straight to the correct platform.

First, I rarely know which platform my train uses until minutes before, meaning I’m hauling bags up and down footbridges based on crackly speaker announcements I can barely hear. 

Second, boards use train names or abbreviations like "Ruhunu Kumari" or "EXP" without plainly stating: "This is an express train to Galle, and it will skip smaller stations." If I accidentally take an express, I’ll miss my stop entirely! 

Third, once I’m standing on the platform, there’s no reassurance. Is the train pulling in right now actually the 3:30 PM to Kandy, or an overdue slow train? 

Also, I shouldn't have to create an account or log in just to check a simple schedule and 2nd class fare. I just want to know: which platform, is it express or slow, how much is the ticket, and am I on the right track?

---

**moderator**

**accessibility-specialist**, when last-minute platform changes occur in a noisy station, what specific multimodal fallback alerts (digital, visual, tactile) must SmartRail mandate so passengers with hearing, visual, or mobility impairments aren't stranded or directed toward inaccessible routes like footbridges?

---

**accessibility-specialist**

When a last-minute platform change happens at Colombo Fort, it’s a crisis for me. If a crackly speaker announces a track shift from Platform 1 to 4, I can't just sprint over a footbridge in my wheelchair.

We must mandate strict multimodal alerts:
1. **Visual & Audio Synchronisation**: Station displays, terminals, and audio announcements must update simultaneously, in plain Sinhala, Tamil, and English. Audio must be converted to visible, high-contrast text on screens for deaf passengers.
2. **Haptic Alerts**: Mobile updates must use distinct vibration patterns and screen-reader accessible text, never using colour alone to signal a change.
3. **Step-Free Path Routing**: A platform change message *must* indicate the accessible, step-free path. If the lift to that platform is broken, sending me there strands me. The system must verify lift status before rerouting me, or immediately alert station staff to assist.

The brief mentions disruption updates on displays, but it completely ignores step-free routing, lift outage tracking, screen-reader standards, and multi-language accessibility. What good is a platform change notification if the only way there is a flight of stairs I cannot climb?

**moderator**

**occasional-commuter**, you mentioned wanting instant, real-time platform updates and train reassurances on your phone without the hassle of creating an account or logging in. If the system doesn't know who you are or what train you plan to take, how do you expect SmartRail to deliver those specific, real-time alerts directly to your personal device as you move through the station?

---

**occasional-commuter**

Look, I don't expect the app to read my mind! What I mean is, I shouldn't have to fill out a registration form, type my email, and set a password while holding two heavy bags in the middle of Colombo Fort. 

Why can't I just open the app as a guest, search "Fort to Kandy", tap the 3:30 PM train, and hit "Track this journey"? That's all! My phone then knows which train I care about for the next two hours without needing my personal details or an account. 

Or even simpler, why not have a poster at the station entrance where I can tap or scan to instantly load today’s live departures for Fort?

Apps today always force you to register first. If I’m rushing to check whether the train on Platform 3 is an express or a slow train, spending three minutes verifying an email account means I miss the train completely. 

The brief mentions that passenger functionality might require an account, but forcing a login just to save a temporary trip for one afternoon is a huge mistake. I just want quick, guest access to follow my ride today!

---

**moderator**

**accessibility-specialist**, the occasional-commuter strongly advocates for an anonymous "guest mode" without user accounts or stored profiles. If a passenger uses the system anonymously, how should SmartRail capture and apply individual accessibility needs—such as step-free routing or screen-reader preferences—in real time without forcing them through an account setup?

---

**accessibility-specialist**

The commuter is absolutely right. Forcing someone to create an account—especially an older uncle with shaky hands or someone trying to navigate Colombo Fort with a visual impairment—is a massive barrier. We must never gate accessibility behind a login screen.

First, the app must respect standard OS accessibility settings. If a passenger already has their phone set to use a screen reader or high-contrast text, the SmartRail app must inherit this automatically in guest mode. 

Second, we need a simple, prominent "Step-Free Routing" toggle right on the very first screen of the guest interface. It takes one tap. No email, no password. Once activated, every search result from Fort to Gampaha will automatically filter for stations with working lifts and ramp-accessible platforms. 

For the public terminals, we must have physical, tactile buttons at a reachable height (under 1.2 metres) that instantly trigger audio-guided step-free routes, or change the display to high-contrast Sinhala, Tamil, or English. 

Accessibility is about removing barriers, not adding digital checkpoints.
