# Session daily-commuter+occasional-commuter-2026-10-04T04-52-59-658Z

Topic: How much guidance should the app show?

**moderator**

Daily Commuter, since you already know your routine route, under what specific conditions would you want the app to switch from showing zero guidance to providing active, step-by-step navigation?

---

**daily-commuter**

Step-by-step navigation? Honestly, almost never. I’ve walked the same platform for five years; I don’t need an app telling me how to walk to Platform 2. That kind of hand-holding is for tourists, and extra screens just get in the way when I’m checking my phone one-handed at the ticket gate.

The *only* time I want the app to step in actively is when my commute goes completely sideways. 

If my usual train is suddenly cancelled, delayed by over fifteen minutes, or hits a last-minute platform swap, that is when I need direct guidance. Don’t just show a vague red delay warning. Tell me immediately: 
1. Which platform is the train moving to, before the whole crowd starts running?
2. If it’s cancelled, what is the *very next* train that actually stops at my station? 
3. Will that replacement train make me miss my kid's school pickup?

On a normal day, keep it out of my face. One tap: is my 7:45 on time, and what platform? That's all I want. But when service collapses, don’t leave me guessing and asking station staff like I have to do today—tell me what to do next.

---

**moderator**

Occasional Commuter, while the daily commuter wants the app to stay completely out of the way on normal days, at what specific decision points—such as navigating inside the station, managing transfers, or finding the right platform—do you actually need active, step-by-step guidance?

---

**occasional-commuter**

I understand regular commuters just want a quick glance, but for someone like me, *every* trip feels unfamiliar. Stripping away guidance might save a commuter one second, but it leaves occasional travellers like me completely stranded!

I need step-by-step reassurance at specific decision points:

1. **Entering the station:** I don’t have the layout memorised. Tell me where my platform is and how to get there—especially if there are stairs or long walkways, because I'm usually lugging a suitcase.
2. **At the platform:** Station abbreviations confuse me. I need plain-language confirmation: *"Yes, this approaching train stops at your destination."* I constantly worry I'll accidentally board an express that skips my stop.
3. **Interchanges:** This is my biggest panic point. If I have to change trains, I need to know: which platform do I arrive at, which platform do I run to, and do I realistically have enough time to drag my bags across the footbridge?
4. **Before arriving:** A simple prompt saying, *"Your stop is next, get ready to step off."* 

When you travel only a few times a month, silence from an app isn't convenient—it’s terrifying.

---

**moderator**

Daily Commuter, for the app to give you proactive disruption alerts and replacement options without you having to plan a journey every morning, how do you expect SmartRail to know which train you are targeting—would you set up a recurring commute profile, or do you expect the app to automatically detect your trip based on location and habits?

---

**daily-commuter**

Definitely a simple recurring commute setting. Set it once and never touch it again. Just let me pick my station pair and usual trains—say, the 7:45 AM into the city and the 5:15 PM back, Monday to Friday. 

Please don't make the app guess based on my location. For one thing, background tracking eats battery, and signal drops completely on parts of my line anyway. But more importantly: automated detection is too late. 

If the app waits until my GPS sees me walking toward the ticket gate, what good is that? If my 7:45 is delayed or cancelled, I need a push notification at 7:10 while I'm still tying my shoes at home. That gives me time to sprint for the 7:25, rearrange my morning, or warn my manager. 

And don't force me through some twenty-question onboarding survey either. Give me a clear "Save as my commute" button on my regular train, with a toggle for push alerts if it's delayed over five minutes or swaps platforms. Let me lock it in once, and don't bother me unless something breaks.

---

**moderator**

Occasional Commuter, to deliver the detailed in-station navigation and timely "step off now" alerts you asked for, how do you expect the app to track your real-time progress—especially in underground stations or low-signal areas where GPS fails?

---

**occasional-commuter**

I honestly don’t know how the technology works behind the scenes, and I really don’t want an app tracking *me* personally or draining my phone battery! 

To be honest, you don't need to track my footsteps—you just need to track the *train*. 

When I look up a trip, I just want to tap "I'm on this train." If SmartRail already knows where the train is, surely the app can figure out when we're approaching my stop? Even if signal drops in a tunnel, having an offline checklist of stops or a simple alert saying, *"Next stop is yours in roughly 5 minutes"* would stop me from constantly panicking and craning my neck to spot blurry platform signs.

And for navigating the station, I don't need fancy indoor GPS. Just give me plain, clear text: *"Platform 3 is past the ticket barriers, down the lift on your right."* 

Whatever you do, if the signal dies underground, please don't let the app freeze on a blank loading screen. Just keep my journey guidance visible so I’m not stuck guessing with heavy bags in the middle of a crowd!
